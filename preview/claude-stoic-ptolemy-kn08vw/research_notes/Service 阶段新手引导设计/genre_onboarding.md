# Genre onboarding patterns for a real-time "service" loop (first day / first level)

Scope note: research was done through web search only. Direct fetches of gamedeveloper.com and amara.org (the GMTK Overcooked transcript) failed because those hosts are blocked in this session's network, so claims from those sources rely on search excerpts and are marked (lower confidence). Most per-game detail comes from fan wikis, walkthroughs and reviews rather than developer postmortems. Where a game in the requested list had no usable result (Coffee Talk, Spiritfarer, Unpacking, Diner Dash first level), that is listed under Gaps rather than filled in from memory.

## Q1. How do genre games handle the first day: scripted tutorial vs learn-as-you-go, what comes first, how many mechanics, frozen/slowed time, mentor, coach marks, text vs show-by-doing?

### Takeaway
The common pattern is a short, mandatory, low-stakes first level built around one dish/one rule, a mentor NPC who gives brief instructions, and then new mechanics added roughly one per day or level. Time pressure is softened at the start (a timer that doesn't start until the first success, warnings without penalty, very easy first level) rather than fully frozen. The designer of Papers, Please deliberately chose "learn on the fly under pressure" plus cheap day replays in place of a formal tutorial.

### Cited Findings
**Overcooked 1/2**
- Both Overcooked and Overcooked 2 open with introductory tutorial levels that are unmissable and must be completed to continue the story. — [PowerPyx trophy guide (search excerpt)](https://www.powerpyx.com/overcooked-all-you-can-eat-trophy-guide-roadmap/)
- Overcooked 1's first real kitchen, Level 1-1, has a single recipe (onion soup) and a 2:30 limit; guides call it "generally easy, even for one player." — [Overcooked Wiki 1-1](https://overcooked.fandom.com/wiki/1-1_(Overcooked!)); [TrueAchievements walkthrough ch.1](https://www.trueachievements.com/game/Overcooked/walkthrough/3)
- Players report that in the original Overcooked 2 the tutorial-level timer did not start until the first successful order was delivered; this was removed in All You Can Eat, and players complained it made the tutorial's top score nearly impossible. (Community reports, lower confidence.) — [Steam discussion 1](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1243830/discussions/0/3768986805993213498); [Steam discussion 2](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1243830/discussions/0/3598968559036075381)
- A Reddit reply says practice mode has no level timer but orders may still time out. (Unverified.) — [r/OvercookedGame via redlib](https://redlib.hbubli.cc/r/OvercookedGame/comments/1jor5h1/is_there_a_way_to_turn_off_timerlower_difficulty)
- Throwing (new in Overcooked 2) is described by one guide as taught in early World 2 rather than the opening level; sources disagree. — [Silicon Era](https://www.siliconera.com/overcooked-2-feels-like-it-fine-tunes-the-experience/); [earlyguides walkthrough](https://earlyguides.com/overcooked-2/walkthrough)
- GMTK's analysis says the first stage's long central island makes moving between onions and stove tedious, i.e. the layout itself teaches that space and travel matter. (From a transcript search excerpt; lower confidence.) — [GMTK transcript on amara (search excerpt)](https://amara.org/subtitles/1kyGmzeQjsv6/en/3/download/How%20Overcookeds%20Kitchens%20Force%20You%20to%20Communicate%20%20Game%20Makers%20Toolkit.en.vtt); [Superjump: How design creates teamwork](https://superjumpmagazine.com/overcooked-how-design-creates-teamwork)

**PlateUp!**
- On first launch the game loads the tutorial; it can be replayed any time from a "?" Tutorial sign in the HQ/warehouse. (Single community source.) — [TouchTapPlay beginner guide](https://www.touchtapplay.com/plateup-beginner-guide-tips-and-tricks/)
- Loop: serve during the day for money/XP, then buy and place appliances at night; you start in an empty room; card choices are forced every few days. — [Common Sense Gamer tips](https://commonsensegamer.com/plateup-tips-tricks/); [shapes.inc guide](https://shapes.inc/guides/plateup-guide)

**Papers, Please**
- Day 1 has a single rule (only Arstotzkan citizens may enter); new conditions arrive almost daily (foreigners allowed with valid papers, then entry ticket, then permit). — [Hardcore Gaming 101](https://www.hardcoregaming101.net/papers-please/); [Mechanics of Magic: Design details](https://mechanicsofmagic.com/2021/06/05/design-details-in-papers-please/)
- The first two citations each day are warnings with no pay penalty. — [Mechanics of Magic](https://mechanicsofmagic.com/2021/06/05/design-details-in-papers-please/) (via search excerpt)
- Lucas Pope wrote that there are good arguments for a formal tutorial but he prefers learning on the fly under pressure, and made replaying a day easy so the day acts as an implicit tutorial. (Dev-blog content seen via search excerpt; lower confidence.) — [TIGSource dev log](https://www.tigsource.com/?p=26251); [Game Developer: Designing the bleak genius of Papers, Please](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/designing-the-bleak-genius-of-i-papers-please-i-) (fetch blocked)

**Good Pizza, Great Pizza**
- A mentor character (Alicante) teaches pizza-making; early chapters act as tutorial levels for pizza-making, customer interaction and business; customer count per day rises as you progress. — [Fandom Post review](https://www.fandompost.com/2020/09/08/good-pizza-great-pizza-switch-review/); [CyberPost: reaching chapter 3](https://cyberpost.co/how-to-reach-chapter-3-pizza/)
- Unclear orders can be clarified by tapping "What?", at a cost to the customer's happiness meter, i.e. help exists but has an in-world price. — [TalkAndroid beginner guide](https://www.talkandroid.com/15864-good-pizza-great-pizza-basic-gameplay-guide/)
- One blog describes the start as "thrown into the deep end," though manageable. — [lootandgrind beginner guide](https://lootandgrind.com/good-pizza-great-pizza-beginners-guide)

**Cook, Serve, Delicious!**
- Gamezebo: the game "accelerates from Day One, and it never looks back." A player found controls "hard to grok at first" but they clicked halfway through level 1. Reviewers advise starting with easy/fast foods; the difficulty curve is driven by which recipes you buy. — [Gamezebo review](https://www.gamezebo.com/reviews/cook-serve-delicious-review/); [Ethan Vu review](https://ethanvu.github.io/post/video-game-reviews/cook-serve-delicious-2012); [Phenixx Gaming review](https://web.phenixxgaming.com/2019/05/09/cook-serve-delicious-pc-review)

**Two Point Hospital**
- First level (Hogsport) has you build reception, GP office, pharmacy (plus staff room, toilet), then hire staff; training is not in level 1 and unlocks around level 3 (Mitton University). — [PC Gamer hands-on](https://www.pcgamer.com/uk/two-point-hospital-hands-on-theme-hospital-fans-are-getting-the-exact-game-they-want-with-a-few-twists/); [SegmentNext Mitton guide](https://segmentnext.com/two-point-hospital-mitton); [GameWatcher training](https://www.gamewatcher.com/two-point-hospital-staff-training)

**Stardew Valley**
- No formal tutorial; brief on-screen prompts appear at wake-up and guide the eye to a "(!)" marker; Day 1 farm is cluttered with no quest marker; first quests are meeting neighbours and harvesting a parsnip. — [mariip edublog analysis](https://mariip.edublogs.org/?p=289); [trialsanderrorsux blog](https://trialsanderrorsux.blogspot.com/2016/03); [TheGamer getting-started](https://www.thegamer.com/stardew-valley-getting-started-guide/)
- Critiques: lack of a proper tutorial makes it hard to start; dropping/trashing/eating items confused a UX reviewer; reliance on an external wiki called "bad design." — [manpaint journal](https://manpaint.neocities.org/journal/stardew-valley.html); [trialsanderrorsux](https://trialsanderrorsux.blogspot.com/2016/03)

**Moonlighter (shop)**
- A brief tutorial by an NPC (Zenon) introduces the shop; customers react with one of four emoji faces (too cheap to too expensive) and players refine prices over sales. Some players complain systems "lack clear explanations, leading to guesswork" (review aggregator, lower confidence). — [PC Gamer preview](https://www.pcgamer.com/in-moonlighter-youre-part-adventurer-part-shopkeeper); [HyperX blog](https://blog.hyperx.com/article/4281/how-much-should-you-sell-items-for-in-moonlighter); [vaporlens summary](https://vaporlens.app/app/606150/moonlighter.md)

**Potion Craft**
- TechRaptor: the start covers what you need, and recurring NPCs explain their importance when you meet them; the guided tutorial lasts until master level I, after which players are on their own. A small dev cites Potion Craft as doing "show, don't tell" well. — [TechRaptor review](https://techraptor.net/gaming/reviews/potion-craft-alchemist-simulator-review); [TechRaptor preview](https://techraptor.net/gaming/previews/potion-craft-alchemist-simulator-preview); [itch.io post](https://itch.io/post/11820607)

**Wilmot's Warehouse**
- The tutorial asks you to sort into "winter" and "hats" and then hands you a woolly hat, i.e. it deliberately teaches that categories are yours to decide. — [Wikipedia](https://wikipedia.com/wiki/Wilmot%27s_Warehouse)

**General principles**
- Nintendo-style sequence: introduce a mechanic in isolation, reinforce with variation, then test with increased challenge. — [NerdyTeachers PICO-8 game design](https://nerdyteachers.com/PICO-8/Game_Design/105); [Wayline](https://www.wayline.io/blog/tutorials-onboarding-level-design)
- "Invisible tutorial" = show-don't-tell via integrated gameplay (e.g. a gap that requires the new jump instead of text explaining it); some mechanics (strategy-game systems) still need text. — [Game Developer: Methods of creating invisible tutorials](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/methods-of-creating-invisible-tutorials) (search excerpt)

### Inferences
- The most consistent genre pattern: day 1 = one recipe/one rule, low or no fail cost, mentor gives 1-line prompts; each later day adds one mechanic (Papers Please, Overcooked worlds, Two Point training at level 3, CSD recipe purchases, GPGP chapters).
- "Freeze time" is rare; softer tools are more common: timer that starts on first success (old Overcooked 2), penalty-free first mistakes (Papers Please warnings), easy capacity (Overcooked 1-1).
- For this game's Service phase, that maps to: day 1 with guests only at staffed tables + one manual table, no tour bus, no courier, no ad guests; then add one element per day (manual seating, rush food, courier, ad guests, events).
- Contextual "first time you see X" NPC explanations (Potion Craft, Moonlighter) are cheap to build in a browser game (one-shot popups keyed to first occurrence).

### Gaps
- No developer postmortem or GDC talk found that explicitly describes Overcooked's, PlateUp's or Diner Dash's first-level onboarding design; Game Developer pages could not be fetched.
- No usable sources on Coffee Talk, Spiritfarer, Unpacking, or Diner Dash's first level (step-by-step) were found.
- Whether Overcooked's tutorial uses arrows/coach marks was not confirmed by any source.

## Q2. How do they introduce a timing/QTE minigame the first time?

### Takeaway
Evidence is thin; the clearest pattern is a separate practice option (Cooking Mama) and lenient time limits so failures are rare, plus visual guides (CSD fill lines). No source confirmed a slowed marker on first attempt.

### Cited Findings
- Cooking Mama lets tasks be practised before attempting them for real; one reviewer practised every step before cooking. — [PocketGamer](https://www.pocketgamer.com/articles/002195/cooking-mama/); [Blogcritics Wii review](https://blogcritics.org/nintendo-wii-review-cooking-mama-world/)
- GamesRadar: every minigame is timed but "you will very rarely fail to beat the time limit"; it's often unclear which minigames allow retries. — [GamesRadar review](https://www.gamesradar.com/uk/cooking-mama/)
- A Wii review says the game gives little tutorial material and "puts you right into the action." — [Blogcritics](https://blogcritics.org/nintendo-wii-review-cooking-mama-world/)
- CSD reviewers recommend using in-game visual guides to avoid over/underfilling; controls take part of the first level to click. — [Ethan Vu review](https://ethanvu.github.io/post/video-game-reviews/cook-serve-delicious-2012)
- It Takes Two softens timing errors by auto-pulling characters to a ledge when close. — [Game Informer review](https://Www.gameinformer.com/review/it-takes-two/it-takes-two-review-the-joys-and-heartbreak-of-love)

### Inferences
- Browser-friendly options: (a) a first-time no-fail rush (miss = retry that step, no wasted time/money), (b) a wider green zone or slower marker on day 1 only, (c) a leniency window like It Takes Two's ledge assist. These are inferences, not documented practice in the cited games.
- Since rush pauses when the chef walks away, first-time teaching should probably happen when no guest is waiting, or the game should hold patience during the first rush.

### Gaps
- No source found describing a slowed-marker or guaranteed-success first QTE in a restaurant game.

## Q3. How do they introduce staff/automation vs manual work?

### Takeaway
Games make players do the job manually first and introduce helpers/automation later as purchasable upgrades (Two Point training at level 3; PlateUp conveyor -> grabber -> smart grabber upgrade chain). No source documents an explicit "watch what the helper does" tutorial.

### Cited Findings
- Two Point Hospital: build and hire basic staff on level 1; staff training is introduced later (around level 3). — [SegmentNext](https://segmentnext.com/two-point-hospital-mitton); [GamersDecide](https://gamersdecide.com/articles/two-point-hospital-best-training)
- Rival hospitals compete for applicants. — [SuperCheats walkthrough](https://www.supercheats.com/two-point-hospital-walkthrough-guide)
- PlateUp automation is an upgrade chain: Conveyor (push only) -> Grabber (pull and push) -> Smart Grabber (trained by placing a sample item) / Rotating Grabber. — [PlateUp official wiki: Automation](https://wiki.plateupgame.com/Automation); [TouchTapPlay automation guide](https://www.touchtapplay.com/how-to-automate-serving-in-plateup-guide-tips/)
- PlateUp's research table is recommended as an early buy because it unlocks key upgrades. — [TouchTapPlay beginner guide](https://www.touchtapplay.com/plateup-beginner-guide-tips-and-tricks/)

### Inferences
- For bunnies: let the player see one staffed rug "doing it automatically" side-by-side with the one table they must handle manually on day 1, so the contrast teaches what staff buy you. Show a brief label/animation on the bunny's first seat action.

### Gaps
- No designer source on why/when helpers are introduced in Diner Dash, Overcooked, or PlateUp.

## Q4. What do developers/critics say worked or failed?

### Takeaway
Praise goes to short, contextual teaching (Potion Craft, Papers Please escalation); complaints target long/confusing tutorials, missing explanations (Stardew, Moonlighter), steep day-1 ramps (CSD), and removing a beginner grace (Overcooked AYCE timer).

### Cited Findings
- Potion Tycoon's tutorial is widely criticized as confusing, overly long, poorly explained and buggy (aggregated Steam reviews; lower confidence). — [vaporlens: Potion Tycoon](https://vaporlens.app/app/1485690/potion_tycoon)
- Overcooked AYCE players objected to the tutorial timer starting immediately. — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1243830/discussions/0/3768986805993213498)
- Papers, Please's compelling element is efficiency built up day by day; the gradual rule rollout is central. — [Ashlander Analysis](https://ashlanderanalysis.substack.com/p/papers-please-a-modern-classic)
- PlateUp keeps the tutorial replayable from the HQ. — [TouchTapPlay](https://www.touchtapplay.com/plateup-beginner-guide-tips-and-tricks/)
- Stardew criticisms of missing tutorial and wiki reliance (see Q1). — [manpaint](https://manpaint.neocities.org/journal/stardew-valley.html)
- Diner Dash: PlayFirst postmortem on sequel direction (not about onboarding). — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/finding-the-game-in-a-sequel-to-a-sequel-to-a-sequel) (search excerpt)

### Inferences
- Make the tutorial short, replayable, and skippable for replayers; keep early mistakes cheap rather than freezing everything.

### Gaps
- No GDC talk on restaurant-game onboarding was retrievable.

## Q5. How do 2-player / co-op games onboard two players at once?

### Takeaway
Co-op games mostly onboard both players together through a shared easy level with simple, consistent controls and frequent new abilities; It Takes Two's first chapter teaches the asymmetric pairing. No source found on competitive side-by-side onboarding like this game's.

### Cited Findings
- Overcooked tutorials are mandatory and played by whoever is present; Level 1-1 is "easy, even for one player." — [PowerPyx](https://www.powerpyx.com/overcooked-all-you-can-eat-trophy-guide-roadmap/); [TrueAchievements](https://www.trueachievements.com/game/Overcooked/walkthrough/3)
- Overcooked's kitchens are designed to force communication. — [Superjump](https://superjumpmagazine.com/overcooked-how-design-creates-teamwork)
- It Takes Two: the first level (the Tree) is described as a tutorial for asymmetric abilities (sap/match); controls are simple and consistent while new gadgets arrive regularly; mechanics change per chapter tied to story. — [earlyguides](https://earlyguides.pages.dev/it-takes-two); [Push Square review](https://pushsquare.com/reviews/ps5/it_takes_two); [Wikipedia mirror](https://kiwix.lema.org/wikipedia_en_all_mini_2024-02/A/It_Takes_Two_(video_game))
- Wilmot's Warehouse has a split-screen 2-player co-op mode. — [Wikipedia](https://wikipedia.com/wiki/Wilmot%27s_Warehouse)

### Inferences
- Since both players see the same day 1, introduce the same single mechanic to both at once and let each finish independently; avoid making one player wait on the other's tutorial.

### Gaps
- No sources found on onboarding in competitive two-player management games; Overcooked/It Takes Two specifics come from fan guides.
